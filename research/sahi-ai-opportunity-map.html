Sahi AI Opportunity Map

How brokers in India and abroad use AI for traders and investors, the rules that shape it, and 46 desktop concepts for Sahi. Includes a deep dive on a live value-chain canvas for fundamentals investors.

Updated 8 Oct 2026 Stage Research, not a build plan Scope web.sahi.com and desktop

Summary

Seven findings from the scan of brokers, research tools and regulators.

  • Explaining price moves is now standardRobinhood Cortex, Public Alpha, eToro Tori, IBKR and Groww GR-1 all explain why a stock moved in plain language. On its own, that won't set Sahi apart.
  • AI agents arrived in 2026, with limitsPublic, eToro, Futu, Webull and Upstox now let AI place trades. Each caps the budget, limits which stocks it can touch, previews orders and has an off switch.
  • Indian brokers are taking two routesZerodha, Kotak and Upstox let users connect their own AI. Groww, Dhan, Angel One and Kotak are building in-house analysts. Nobody has AI built for active F&O traders, Sahi's core users.
  • The value-chain canvas fills a real gapSupply-chain maps exist only in professional terminals like Bloomberg and FactSet. This scan found no retail product. The 2021 chip shortage and 2025 rare-earth curbs show the need in Indian auto.
  • Trader discipline is the biggest gapOver 91% of individual F&O traders lost money in FY25, about ₹1.06 lakh crore in total. Brokers barely use AI to protect traders from themselves.
  • Regulation sets the boundarySEBI's AI rules are close and call for human oversight and kill switches. AI that says "buy X" counts as regulated advice. The safe ground is AI that explains, compares, simulates and protects.
  • Desktop is where AI goes deepestRobinhood Legend, IBKR Desktop, Bloomberg ASKB and eToro's new desktop platform put AI inside dense, multi-panel work: canvases, command bars, right-click explain, saved workflows. An n8n-style canvas fits naturally here.

Where Sahi starts

Sahi already has trader DNA and a detection layer. The gaps are the layers that turn a signal into understanding.

Already there

  • Flat ₹10 per order, aimed at F&O and intraday traders
  • Web platform at web.sahi.com, plus Android and iOS apps
  • 1-click chart trading, trailing stop-loss, Scalper Mode
  • 20+ real-time scanners: volume shockers, breakouts, gap up/down, golden and death cross, 52-week high/low
  • Option chain with payoff graphs and Greeks; GTT, GTC and basket orders
  • Sahi AI flags breakouts, trend shifts and unusual activity in real time

Layers that are missing

  • Explanation. Why did it move?
  • Context. What usually happens next, and how does it affect my positions?
  • Discipline. Protecting traders from revenge trading, overtrading and rule drift.
  • Investor layer. Fundamentals, theses and value chains for people who don't trade off charts.

Patterns across the market

  • P1Explaining moves is table stakes. Digests and "why it moved" are everywhere.
  • P2Chat becomes an object. The best products turn a prompt into a scan, indicator, strategy or order card the user can edit.
  • P3Interpretation comes before action. Webull voice preview, Groww order card, Public's follow-up questions.
  • P4Autonomy has explicit limits. Budgets, symbol lists, caps, kill switches, per-tool consent.
  • P5Proactive alerts beat reactive chat. Personalised "this matters to you" pushes.
  • P6Memory and profiles. eToro remembers sessions; Bloomberg stores role and focus.
  • P7India opens up through MCP. Zerodha, Kotak and Upstox let users bring Claude.
  • P8Two areas are under-served. Trader discipline, and supply-chain reasoning for retail.

Rules and risks that shape the design

Indian regulation and the loss data both point to AI that explains and protects, rather than AI that pushes more trades.

ConstraintWhat it saysDesign implication
SEBI F&O study FY25Over 91% of individual F&O traders made net losses. Aggregate net loss about ₹1,05,603 crore, up 41% on FY24. FY22–24: 93% lost, over ₹1.8 lakh crore.AI that raises trading frequency is risky for users and reputation. AI that protects traders is strong positioning.
SEBI AI/ML guidelines due soonConsultation June 2025. In Aug 2026 the chairman said rules are coming shortly: human oversight, kill switches, tiered approach. The regulated entity is accountable for AI it uses, including third-party tools.Visible AI labels, human-in-the-loop for actions, kill switch, audit logs, model testing.
Intermediaries amendment 2025Adds a chapter on AI use.Same as above.
Research analyst and adviser rulesSince the Dec 2024 amendment, RAs and IAs must disclose how much they use AI tools. "Trading calls" are formally defined.AI that says "buy X" is regulated advice. Keep AI explaining, comparing and simulating, unless calls go through a registered RA.
Retail algo framework mandatory 1 Apr 2026Every algo order carries an exchange Algo-ID. The broker is principal; providers are agents. Strategies above 10 orders per second must be registered. Static IP for API use."Describe a strategy and let AI run it" must route through this framework.
Exchange AI/ML reportingTrading members file half-yearly AI/ML disclosures.Keep an inventory of AI features.
IOSCO Mar 2025Flags over-reliance on AI, concentration on a few providers and human-AI interaction risk. Retail herding is a stated concern.Don't serve everyone the same AI pick.
Trust data31% of US investors would act on AI financial advice without verifying it (CFP Board, 2023).Users may over-trust AI. Design for verification: citations, confidence, base rates.

Deep dive: the value-chain canvas

Fundamentals investors reason in causes: inputs, capacity, demand, margins. A value-chain canvas makes that reasoning visible and live. Each link in the chain is a node that changes colour when its signals change, the way n8n shows data moving through a workflow.

  • The need is recent. In Sept 2021 Maruti guided to about 40% of normal output because of the chip shortage. July–September profit fell 66% and about 1.16 lakh vehicles went unbuilt. In 2025, China's rare-earth curbs cut Bajaj Chetak output by about half in July.
  • Signals showed up before the stock moved. Bajaj warned about magnet supply on its May 2025 earnings call, weeks before production was hit.
  • Auto is a good first sector. It discloses suppliers and customers more than most sectors do.
  • No retail competitor. Today this view exists only in professional terminals.

Each node carries

  • State. Normal, watch, stress or disrupted, driven by commodity prices, import data, news, management commentary and peer filings.
  • Listed companies at this node, with the user's holdings highlighted.
  • Evidence drawer. The news, filings and concall quotes behind the state, dated and sourced.

Each link carries

  • Strength. Share of cost or revenue exposed, where disclosed.
  • Substitutability. Alternate sources and how easy switching is.
  • Lag. How long a shock takes to arrive, given inventory and lead times.
  • Provenance. Disclosed (solid) or AI-inferred (dashed), with a confidence level.

Same canvas, other lenses

  • Macro transmissionRepo rate to bank margins to NBFC lending to auto loans to two-wheeler demand. Crude to oil marketers, paints, tyres, aviation. Monsoon to rural income to tractors and FMCG. Rupee to IT and pharma exporters.
  • Policy and BudgetA scheme, duty or tax change, with who gains, who loses and by roughly how much.
  • ThemesEV, defence, railways, data centres, semiconductors. The chain doubles as discovery: which listed company makes this part?
  • CompetitionPeers, substitutes and new entrants around a company.
  • News spilloversOne company's news re-prices linked firms. An AI product launch in Feb 2026 hit data and analytics stocks that had no part in it.
  • Other sectorsPharma and China API dependence; IT and US client budgets; FMCG and palm oil; cement and coal; aviation and fuel or engine supply.

Hard problems to research

  • Data completeness. Supply-chain disclosure is thin. The US only requires naming customers above 10% of revenue, and India has no full mandate.
  • Inferred links. Users need to see what is disclosed and what is guessed, without clutter.
  • Correlation vs causation. Use ranges, lags and "exposed to" language, never "will fall".
  • Staleness. Chain structure changes slowly; node states change daily. Refresh them on different cycles.
  • Cognitive load. Real chains are hairballs. Use progressive disclosure and a "show only what changed" mode.
  • The advice line. A list of exposed companies can read as a recommendation, so it needs careful framing.

Desktop interface patterns

Desktop users have screen space, a keyboard and often a second monitor. These patterns make use of that.

  • Canvas workspacen8n-style nodes for value chains, strategies (condition to action) and research boards linking notes, charts and AI findings.
  • Command paletteCtrl K to navigate, ask and act from the keyboard. Active traders live on the keyboard.
  • Right-click explainExplain, compare, alert or add to thesis on any candle, option-chain cell, holding row or node.
  • Side-panel copilot with memoryKeeps context across sessions and learns the user's style.
  • Layouts set up by intent"Set up for Bank Nifty expiry scalping" arranges the 1-minute chart, option chain, OI, positions and P&L.
  • Saved AI workflowsRe-run "my Friday portfolio review" with one click, as Bloomberg ASKB does.
  • Chat that produces objectsEvery answer can become a scan, alert, strategy, chart or basket the user edits directly.
  • Second-screen pop-outsMove the copilot, canvas or journal to another monitor.
  • Hindi, Hinglish and voicePlain-language explanations in the user's language. Angel One and Kotak are already moving here.

Open questions for user research

What to test before choosing a direction.

Investors and the canvas

  1. Do fundamentals investors want to understand the chain themselves, or only hear when something breaks?
  2. How much do they trust AI-inferred links? Does one dashed link lower trust in the whole canvas?
  3. Which start feels natural: company first, or shock first?
  4. How deep can the chain go before it becomes a hairball?
  5. Is this worth paying for, or is it a free engagement driver?

Traders and discipline

  1. Will active traders accept friction such as cool-downs and loss locks? Must they set it themselves?
  2. Where does a helpful nudge become nagging?
  3. Do traders want a live narrator in market hours, or only reviews after the session?

Both

  1. Chat, canvas or command bar: which mode suits which job?
  2. How much citation makes AI feel trustworthy without slowing users down?
  3. English only, or Hindi and Hinglish too? Is voice useful on desktop?
  4. How do users feel about AI acting for them? Which limits make it feel safe?

Suggested methods: contextual inquiry with 5–8 F&O traders and 5–8 fundamentals investors; a diary study through one results season; concept tests of a static value-chain canvas for one carmaker; card sorting of the catalogue by perceived value.
